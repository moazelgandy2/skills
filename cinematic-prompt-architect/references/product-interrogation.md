# Product Interrogation & Brand DNA Extraction Playbook

Top creative agencies (Ogilvy, Droga5, Wieden+Kennedy, BBDO) do not begin campaign ideation with generic brainstorming. They begin with **Product Interrogation** — a disciplined, forensic cross-examination of the product, its engineering, its material truth, and the human condition it touches.

---

## 1. The Philosophy of Product Interrogation

> *"Advertising people who ignore research are as dangerous as generals who ignore decodes of enemy signals."* — David Ogilvy

A product interrogation is not an administrative intake form. It is the first phase of creative strategy. The goal is to shatter superficial marketing buzzwords ("revolutionary", "seamless", "innovative") and uncover:
1. **The Mechanical Reality**: How the product is actually constructed, formulated, or engineered.
2. **The Undeniable Competitive White Space**: The one factual or emotional truth about the product that no competitor can claim without lying.
3. **The Human Condition**: What emotional tension, secret shame, status desire, or daily friction this product actually resolves.

---

## 2. The "So What?" Drill (Feature-to-Human-Truth Translation)

Clients always lead with features. Strategists must ruthlessly drill through functional claims until they hit genuine emotional and psychological bedrock:

```
[Level 1: Functional Feature]
"Our app automates invoice chasing with one-click automated email reminders."
           │
           ▼ (Ask: "So what?")
[Level 2: Practical Advantage]
"Freelancers and agency owners don't have to manually write awkward payment reminder emails."
           │
           ▼ (Ask: "So what does that actually mean for their daily life?")
[Level 3: Emotional Friction & Social Tension]
"They don't have to feel like a desperate beggar chasing money they legitimately earned."
           │
           ▼ (Ask: "So what is the deep human truth?")
[Level 4: Human Core Identity & Truth]
"Professional dignity and relief from financial anxiety. The app preserves their self-respect and client relationships."
```

### The 4 Levels of Interrogation:
- **Level 1: The Mechanic (What it is)**: Specs, ingredients, software code, battery life, fabric weave.
- **Level 2: The Action (What it does)**: Speeds up X, removes Y, strengthens Z.
- **Level 3: The Emotional Relief (What it stops)**: Eliminates embarrassment, saves sleep, prevents conflict, removes cognitive overload.
- **Level 4: The Identity Payoff (Who they become)**: The confident professional, the relaxed parent, the admired pioneer, the unburdened creator.

## 3. The 6-Question Interrogation Matrix (One-by-One Dialogue)

When running the intake interview, ask these questions one at a time:

### Q1. The Mechanical Truth & Formulation
- *"What is this product made of, how does it physically work, and what happens behind the scenes that the customer never sees?"*
- *Look for*: Boring technical facts that can become visual gold (e.g. cold-forged aircraft-grade aluminum, a dual-layer ceramic heating coil, 48 hours of slow-fermented dough).

### Q2. The Competitive White Space (The Unclaimable Truth)
- *"What is the one thing your product does or stands for that your biggest competitor CANNOT say without getting sued or laughed out of the room?"*
- *Look for*: The unfair advantage, the unique mechanism, the bold guarantee, the polar opposite philosophy.

### Q3. The Real Enemy
- *"What is this ad truly fighting against? (Not just another brand, but an outdated habit, an industry lie, an exhausting routine, or a hidden societal expectation?)"*
- *Look for*: The villain. Every great ad has an antagonist (e.g. Dove fought "toxic cosmetic perfectionism"; Apple fought "grey corporate conformity"; Liquid Death fought "boring corporate wellness").

### Q4. The Customer's Secret Moment of Pain
- *"Describe the exact physical moment when someone needs this product most. Where are they sitting or standing, what is on their screen or in their hands, and what thought makes them sigh or curse under their breath?"*
- *Look for*: Sensory, relatable scene setting for the 0–3s hook.

### Q5. The Business Skeleton & Campaign Objective
- *"What is the singular business job this video must perform? Are we driving cold-traffic immediate purchases (Direct Response / CAC), establishing premium brand prestige, triggering viral social shares, or explaining a complex new offer?"*
- *Look for*: Metric focus (CPA, CTR, Hold Rate, Brand Sentiment).

### Q6. The Non-Negotiables & Brand DNA
- *"What are the strict boundaries — mandatory brand colors, tagline, banned words, or tone of voice (e.g. self-deprecating humor vs. cinematic luxury vs. urgent whistleblower)?"*
- *Look for*: Guardrails that keep the creative grounded.

## 4. Red Flags & How to Re-Direct

| Client Says (Vague/Cliché) | The Strategist's Redirection |
| :--- | :--- |
| "Our target audience is everyone aged 18–65." | *"If you sell to everyone, you appeal to no one. Who is the single person who is in so much pain right now that they would buy this at 2:00 AM on a credit card?"* |
| "Our product is high quality and affordable." | *"Every brand on earth claims quality and value. Tell me the specific part of the product that cost you the most sleepless nights to get right."* |
| "We just want a cool viral video." | *"Virality is a byproduct of cultural provocation or extreme empathy. What controversial truth about your industry are you willing to say out loud?"* |
