# Campaign Blueprints: Strategy, Hooks & Scamp Storyboards

This reference provides the operational structures, copywriting formulas, hook taxonomies, and storyboard architectures required to turn raw creative ideas into production-ready Campaign Blueprints.

---

## 1. Strategic Copywriting Frameworks

### The Single-Minded Proposition (SMP)
- **Definition**: The single most persuasive, memorable statement that can be made about the product. If the viewer remembers only one sentence from a 30-second commercial, this is it.
- **Formula**: `[Brand/Product] is the only [Category] that [Unique Mechanism] so that you can [Primary Human Truth/Relief].`
- *Example*: "Notion is the only workspace that connects notes, docs, and project management in one flexible canvas so you can finally stop drowning in 15 disconnected browser tabs."

### Key Consumer Benefit (KCB)
- **Distinction**: Features describe the object; benefits describe the user's life after the object.
- **Formula**: `Feature: [What it has] ──► Benefit: [What it does] ──► Emotional Payoff: [How it makes you feel].`

### Eugene Schwartz's 5 Stages of Market Awareness
Tailor the video ad's opening structure to the audience's psychological state:

| Awareness Stage | Audience Mindset | Video Ad Strategic Approach | Opening Hook Style |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Unaware** | Don't know they have a problem; blissfully ignorant. | Tell a relatable human story or present an intriguing cultural anomaly. | Relatable human friction or surreal pattern interrupt. |
| **2. Problem Aware** | Feel the pain, but don't know a category of solution exists. | Agitate the pain point intensely; validate their frustration. | "Stop doing [painful habit]" or "Nobody warns you about [pain]". |
| **3. Solution Aware** | Know solutions exist, but don't know your specific product. | Differentiate your unique mechanism from old, broken methods. | "Why traditional [competitor method] always fails you." |
| **4. Product Aware** | Know your product, but haven't bought yet (skeptical). | Provide overwhelming proof, extreme demonstration, or social validation. | Extreme stress test, side-by-side split screen comparison. |
| **5. Most Aware** | Know you, want it, just need the trigger to act. | Present an urgent offer, new variation, or direct call to action. | "Our biggest release of the year just dropped." |

## 2. The 8 High-Performing 0–3s Hook Architectures

In short-form video and social advertising, **the first 3 seconds dictate 80% of campaign ROAS**. Viewers swipe at the slightest hint of boredom. Use these proven hook frameworks:

### 1. The "Nobody Warns You" Hook
- *Acoustic*: Whispered confessional tone + intimate room tone.
- *Visual*: Medium close-up, leaning toward camera, serious gaze.
- *Script*: "Nobody warns you about what happens to your [body/business/sleep] after [common milestone]..."

### 2. The Contrarian Reversal Hook
- *Acoustic*: Sudden sharp transient hit or complete audio silence.
- *Visual*: A character doing the exact opposite of common sense (e.g. throwing a brand-new running shoe into a mud pit).
- *Script*: "Everything your [trainer/accountant/dentist] told you about [topic] is completely backwards."

### 3. The Extreme Visual Demonstration Hook
- *Acoustic*: Hyper-tactile Foley (metal grinding, glass fracturing, pneumatic hiss).
- *Visual*: An immediate, jaw-dropping torture test (e.g. dragging a suitcase behind a speeding truck).
- *Script*: Zero speech in the first 2 seconds; pure visual spectacle followed by: "Let's see if this actually survives."

### 4. The Relatable Pain Confession (POV)
- *Acoustic*: Sigh of exhaustion, audible breath catch.
- *Visual*: POV framing of a messy room, chaotic desk, or crying toddler.
- *Script*: "POV: It's 2:45 PM on a Tuesday and you're already on your fourth cup of burnt coffee just to survive..."

### 5. The Before-and-After Shock Hook
- *Acoustic*: Dramatic riser cutting abruptly to dead silence on the reveal.
- *Visual*: Rapid 0.5s flicker between the disastrous starting state and the flawless finished state.
- *Script*: "This took 15 minutes. Look at where we started..."

### 6. The "Stop Doing This" Mistake Hook
- *Acoustic*: Warning buzzer, record scratch, or blunt vocal address.
- *Visual*: Direct-to-camera pointer gesture, freeze frame with high-contrast text overlay.
- *Script*: "If you're still [common habit], you need to stop immediately. Here's why."

### 7. The Negative Consequence / Nightmare Hook
- *Acoustic*: Low drone, clock ticking down.
- *Visual*: A character realizing a catastrophic mistake in slow motion.
- *Script*: "This is the single most expensive mistake every [job title / persona] makes in their first year."

### 8. The Sensory ASMR / Tactile Shock Hook
- *Acoustic*: Hyper-intimate 10cm mic placement, crisp transient clicks, peeling plastic, fluid pouring.
- *Visual*: Macro optical focus (100mm lens) exploring fine textures and material finishes.
- *Script*: No spoken words for 4 seconds; pure physical tactile satisfaction.

## 3. The 5-Beat Video Ad Scamp Storyboard Architecture

Every commercial concept must be structured across five dramatic beats that balance psychological persuasion with cinematic pacing:

```
[0.0s – 3.0s]  Beat 1: The Hook & Pattern Interrupt (Stop the Scroll)
[3.0s – 7.0s]  Beat 2: Problem Agitation & Friction (Validate the Pain)
[7.0s – 11.0s] Beat 3: The Pivot & Product Discovery (The Inevitable Solution)
[11.0s – 15.0s] Beat 4: Empirical Proof & Transformation (Satisfying Validation)
[15.0s – 20.0s+] Beat 5: The Payoff & Call to Action (The Resolved Future)
```

### Detailed Beat Breakdown:
- **Beat 1: Scroll-Stopping Hook (0.0s – 3.0s)**:
  - Visual: High-motion anomaly, unusual angle, or provocative text banner.
  - Audio: Acoustic shock, silence drop, or piercing question.
  - Job: Earn the viewer's next 5 seconds.
- **Beat 2: Problem Agitation / Friction (3.0s – 7.0s)**:
  - Visual: Physicalization of the obstacle (cluttered desk, aching back, spinning loading wheel).
  - Audio: Anxious tension bed, subtle discordant drone, character sigh.
  - Job: Make the problem feel unbearable and relatable.
- **Beat 3: The Pivot & Product Discovery (7.0s – 11.0s)**:
  - Visual: Elegant, cinematic introduction of the product/hero. Camera shifts from chaotic handheld to smooth, stable tracking.
  - Audio: Harmonic resolution, clear mechanical Foley of the product activating.
  - Job: Introduce the hero not as an ad, but as relief.
- **Beat 4: Empirical Proof & Transformation (11.0s – 15.0s)**:
  - Visual: Direct demonstration of the mechanism working; visible character relief and aesthetic satisfaction.
  - Audio: Upbeat pacing motor, bright high-frequency sheen, crisp tactile Foley.
  - Job: Destroy skepticism with indisputable visual evidence.
- **Beat 5: Climax & Call to Action (15.0s – 20.0s+)**:
  - Visual: Confident hero framing, brand mark mnemonic, simple clean offer terms.
  - Audio: Signature sonic brand logo / mnemonic sting, clear vocal CTA delivery.
  - Job: Tell the viewer exactly what to do next without friction.
