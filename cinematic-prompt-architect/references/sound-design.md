# Cinematic Sound Design & Acoustic Theory Bible

Bad AI-video audio is almost never a model limit — it is an underspecified layer. Half of the perceived production value, emotional tension, and sensory presence in cinema lives in sound. Sound actively guides the eye, establishes physical scale, masks synthetic visual artifacts, and binds the viewer into a visceral, believable reality. This reference establishes the theoretical, perceptual, and acoustic foundations for prompting sound like a master supervising sound editor.

---

## 1. Film Sound Semiotics & Audio-Vision (Michel Chion)

Cinema is not image plus sound; it is **Audio-Vision** — a unified cognitive phenomenon where sound redefines what the viewer sees.

### Synchresis (Synchronism + Synthesis)
- **The Axiom**: The spontaneous, irresistible cognitive fusion forged between an acoustic phenomenon and a simultaneous visual phenomenon.
- **Why it matters**: The human brain will accept virtually any sound as the "true" cause of an on-screen visual event as long as the temporal attack synchronizes within ~30–50ms. A metallic scrape playing exactly as a character's eyes narrow transforms a subtle glance into psychic threat. Prompt tight temporal alignment between visual impacts and sonic transients to exploit synchresis.

### Acousmatic Sound vs. Visualized Sound
- **Acousmatic Sound**: Audio whose originating source is entirely off-screen or concealed from sight.
  - *Dramatic Function*: Acousmatic sound expands the perceived universe far beyond the 16:9 or 9:16 frame. It induces tension, paranoia, and curiosity because the human auditory system is an omnidirectional early-warning threat detector.
- **The Acousmêtre**: A disembodied voice-character heard continuously from off-screen (e.g. intercom voice, unseen interrogator, stalker outside a window). The acousmêtre holds mysterious power, omnipresence, and psychological dominance until visualized.
- **De-acousmatization**: The exact moment the camera pivots or cuts to reveal the physical origin of an acousmatic sound. This immediately strips the source of its mythical omniscience and grounds it in mundane physical reality. Always specify whether an off-screen sound remains acousmatic or gets de-acousmatized within the shot.

### Empathetic vs. Anempathetic Sound
- **Empathetic Sound**: Music and sound design that directly mimic and amplify the emotional trajectory of the scene (e.g. weeping accompanied by mournful cellos, terror accompanied by screeching strings).
- **Anempathetic Sound**: Sound or music that exhibits conspicuous, cold indifference to the dramatic situation taking place on screen.
  - *Cinematic Power*: Anempathetic sound is 10x more chilling and psychologically devastating than empathetic sound. Examples: an upbeat, cheerful retro radio pop jingle playing over a fatal car crash; a rhythmic, squeaking ceiling fan turning placidly in a room with a corpse; the continuous cheerful chirping of spring birds over a soldier dying in the mud.

### Added Value (*Valeur Ajoutée*) & Temporalization
- **Added Value**: The expressive and informative value with which sound enriches an image, tricking the spectator into believing they "saw" physical weight, velocity, or material hardness that was actually communicated solely through the auditory channel.
- **Temporalization**: Sound imposes tempo, rhythm, and forward velocity on the image. A slow-moving or static visual becomes urgent, erratic, or suspended based entirely on the acoustic rhythm underneath it.

### Diegetic Ontologies: The 4 Zones
1. **Objective External Diegetic**: Physical sounds existing within the story world, audible to all characters present (dialogue, footsteps, rain, engine roar).
2. **Subjective Internal Diegetic (Meta-Diegetic)**: Sounds originating inside a specific character's mind or physical body, reflecting altered psychological states:
   - High-pitched 4kHz–8kHz sine tinnitus after a concussive blast.
   - Low-pass filtered, muffled underwater acoustics during severe emotional shock or panic.
   - Amplified, hyper-intimate bio-acoustics: booming cardiac pulse, blood rushing in the carotid artery, rapid dry gasps.
3. **Trans-Diegetic Sound**: Sound that begins in one ontological domain and morphs into another across a cut or camera move (e.g., a diegetic song playing from a tinny dashboard radio swells in volume, expands into a wide stereo field, and transforms into the full non-diegetic cinematic score for the next scene).
4. **Non-Diegetic**: External score, omniscient voiceover narration, or stylized sound design stingers existing outside the story world, heard only by the audience.

## 2. Acoustic Architecture & Perceptual Limits (Walter Murch)

### The Rule of 2.5 (The Law of Three)
The human conscious mind cannot track more than **two to three distinct sonic events** simultaneously. When a soundscape introduces four or more competing foreground sounds at equal loudness, the brain ceases tracking individual sources and perceives the mix as unstructured noise or "acoustic mud."
- **Master Layering Formula**: In any given frame or beat, strictly budget the mix:
  1. **Primary Anchor (~60% attention)**: Usually spoken dialogue, an operative weapon transient, or a key musical lead.
  2. **Secondary Context (~25% attention)**: Critical diegetic Foley interaction (footstep on gravel, cloth friction, drink glass scrape).
  3. **Tertiary Acoustic Bed (~15% attention)**: The spatial environment / room tone that anchors the scene to physical reality.
- If a fourth element enters (e.g. a musical swell), one of the other elements must immediately step down in level, frequency, or density.

### Dense Clarity
Sensory density must never compromise acoustic transparency. Achieve dense clarity not by piling on continuous sounds, but through **staggered sequential micro-events**: let the shoe heel click land, let the cloth rustle follow on the weight transfer, let the ambient foghorn sound in the silence between dialogue lines.

### Worldizing
Real sound does not exist in an abstract digital vacuum. "Worldizing" is the practice of embedding sound into the physical acoustics, resonant surfaces, and speaker characteristics of the environment (e.g. dialogue played through a cracked radio speaker inside an echoey tiled bathroom). Always specify the acoustic transmission medium.

## 3. Auditory Scene Analysis (Albert Bregman)

The human auditory system parses complex soundscapes through **Auditory Scene Analysis (ASA)**:

### Stream Segregation vs. Spectral Fusion
- **Spectral Fusion**: Acoustic components that share synchronous attack times, common pitch harmonicity, and shared spatial origin are "fused" by the brain into a single perceived object (e.g., combining a wooden crack transient, a sub-bass thump, and a metal rattle into a single "heavy door slam").
- **Stream Segregation**: The brain separates distinct acoustic layers based on differences in frequency range, spatial panning, and temporal onset.
  - *To keep dialogue intelligible*: Never place instruments or sound effects with heavy energy in the **1 kHz – 3 kHz** range at the exact moment a character speaks. Always frequency-carve or temporally offset competing layers.

## 4. Acoustic Ecology & Soundscape Taxonomy (R. Murray Schafer)

Every location has a sonic signature composed of three ecological tiers:

### 1. Keynote Sounds (The Environmental Ground Tone)
- Pervasive, ambient background sounds created by geography, climate, and infrastructure.
- They are the subconscious acoustic anchor of a space: ocean surf on a headland, the low 60Hz hum of high-voltage transformers in an alleyway, wind whistling through a dry pine canopy, continuous HVAC rumble in a corporate high-rise.
- Keynotes are rarely consciously noticed until they stop, but their presence immediately tells the brain where the camera is located.

### 2. Sound Signals (Foreground Informative Events)
- Consciously attended acoustic messages: church bells tolling the hour, emergency vehicle wail, train horn at an open crossing, whistle, telephone ring.

### 3. Soundmarks (Acoustic Landmarks)
- Unique, culturally or geographically specific sounds that define a particular community or setting: the distinctive mechanical chime of a Parisian subway door, the deep steam whistle of a specific harbor ferry, the acoustic resonance of a wooden drawbridge.

### Hi-Fi vs. Lo-Fi Soundscapes
- **Hi-Fi Soundscape** (High Signal-to-Noise Ratio): Pristine, quiet environments (arctic tundra, mountain desert, deep nocturnal forest, carpeted library). Discrete acoustic events can be heard across great distances with pristine clarity; subtle micro-textures (dry leaf crunch, distant breathing) carry immense dramatic weight.
- **Lo-Fi Soundscape** (Low Signal-to-Noise Ratio): Dense urban or industrial spaces (Times Square, trading floor, steel refinery). A high ambient noise floor masks individual signals into a homogeneous acoustic fog. In Lo-Fi scenes, sounds must be physically intimate (tight mic proximity) to cut through the din.

## 5. Architectural Acoustics & Spatial Physics

Believable sound requires physically accurate room behavior:

### Reverberation Time ($RT_{60}$)
The time required for sound energy to drop by 60 dB after the source ceases:
| Space Type | $RT_{60}$ Range | Acoustic Character | Prompting Descriptors |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Anechoic / Heavy Deadening** | < 0.15s | Completely dry, oppressive, intimate | "Zero wall reflections, dead vocal isolation, hyper-dry intimacy" |
| **Furnished Domestic Room** | 0.3s – 0.5s | Controlled, warm, soft boundary absorption | "Carpeted absorption, muffled domestic warmth, tight boxy room" |
| **Classroom / Small Office** | 0.6s – 0.9s | Mild flutter echo, drywall reflection | "Drywall slapback, fluorescent light hum, moderate speech ring" |
| **Industrial Warehouse / Gym** | 1.8s – 3.0s | Cavernous, hollow, metallic flutter | "Cavernous concrete decay, long metallic slap, diffuse reverberant tail" |
| **Cathedral / Wet Stone Tunnel** | 4.0s – 8.0s+ | Massive diffuse wash, speech unintelligibility | "Endless stone resonance, muddy diffuse wash, lingering 6-second tail" |

### Early Reflections vs. Late Diffuse Reverberation
- **Early Reflections (5ms – 30ms)**: The first sound waves bouncing off immediate walls, floors, and ceilings. Early reflections tell the brain the **size, geometry, and boundary materials** of the room.
- **Late Diffuse Reverberation (>50ms)**: The chaotic, scattered decay tail that communicates emotional scale and grandeur.

### Boundary Absorption Coefficients ($\alpha$)
- Hard, reflective surfaces ($\alpha \approx 0.01–0.03$: polished marble, wet asphalt, subway tile, steel bulkheads) produce harsh high-frequency flutter echoes and sharp reflections.
- Soft, absorptive surfaces ($\alpha \approx 0.6–0.9$: heavy velour drapery, deep snowdrifts, dense forest loam, acoustic insulation) absorb high frequencies rapidly, creating intimate, dry, enclosed spaces.

### Distance Physics: Atmospheric HF Attenuation & Inverse Square Law
- **Inverse Square Law ($1/r^2$)**: Acoustic energy drops by 6 dB for every doubling of distance.
- **Atmospheric Air Absorption**: As sound travels through air, high frequencies (>4 kHz) attenuate exponentially faster than low frequencies due to molecular relaxation and humidity.
  - *Prompting Distance*: A distant explosion (1 km away) is **NOT** a quiet version of a close explosion. It is a muffled, low-frequency pressure wave (sub-bass boom <120Hz) with zero high-end crackle or snap. Prompt distance using low-pass filtering and diffused reflection rather than just low volume.

### Spatial Audio & Localization
- **ITD (Interaural Time Difference)** & **ILD (Interaural Level Difference)**: Sound reaching one ear ~0.6ms earlier and louder allows horizontal azimuth localization.
- **HRTF (Head-Related Transfer Function)**: Pinna reflections create spectral notches that tell the brain whether a sound is above, below, in front, or behind the head (e.g. a 4kHz notch indicates an acoustic source directly behind the listener).
- **Doppler Shift**: Compressed high frequencies as an acoustic source accelerates toward the camera; sudden pitch-drop and elongated low frequencies as it passes.

## 6. Psychoacoustics & Cognitive Manipulation

Directly target the viewer's subconscious neurology through acoustic phenomena:

### 1. The Shepard Tone & Shepard-Risset Glissando
- An auditory illusion composed of stacked sine waves spaced an octave apart, continuously sweeping upward while fading out at high frequencies and fading in at low frequencies.
- *Neurological Impact*: Triggers the perception of **infinite, unresolved escalation**. The brain anticipates a climax that never arrives, inducing sustained dread, vertigo, or claustrophobia (used in *Dunkirk*, *The Dark Knight*).

### 2. Infrasound (17 Hz – 19 Hz: The "Fear Frequency")
- Frequencies below the conscious human hearing threshold (<20 Hz) that are felt as physical visceral vibration.
- *Neurological Impact*: 18 Hz closely matches the natural resonant frequency of the human eyeball and inner ear cavity. Infrasonic pressure waves induce subconscious anxiety, hyperventilation, cold chills, dizziness, and optical vibration without the viewer consciously recognizing a sound is playing.

### 3. Equal-Loudness Contours (Fletcher-Munson Curves)
- The human ear is non-linear: it is hyper-sensitive to mid frequencies between **2 kHz and 5 kHz** (the acoustic band of human speech consonants, cracking bone, and infant distress cries) and relatively insensitive to extreme bass (<80Hz) and extreme treble (>12kHz) at quiet volumes.
- *Mix Discipline*: To make a sound piercing and alarm-inducing, boost the 2.5kHz–4kHz band. To make a sound warm and non-threatening, roll off this presence peak.

### 4. The Auditory Looming Effect
- The human brain's evolutionary threat detection treats an approaching acoustic source (rising volume + brightening high-frequency spectral tilt) with extreme urgency, triggering adrenaline before the visual fully resolves.

### 5. The Pre-Drop (Silence as a Weapon)
- Dynamic contrast is relative. A 100 dB explosion following continuous 90 dB noise feels weak.
- Plunging the entire soundscape into **absolute, dead zero-dB silence** for 150ms–300ms immediately prior to an impact, gunshot, or pivotal dialogue whisper makes the subsequent event perceive as 10x louder and more shocking without causing digital clipping or distortion.

## 7. Human Voice Performance Engineering

Synthetic AI dialogue sounds robotic when it lacks the physiological turbulence of the human vocal tract:

### The Respiration & Glottal Cycle
- **Pre-Speech Inhalation**: A natural vocal performance ALWAYS begins with an audible intake of air through the nose or parted lips 150ms–300ms before phonation.
- **Glottal Separation**: Dry, subtle lip-unsticking clicks and glottal pop on vowel initialization.
- **Exhalation Under Speech**: Laughter must land on an exhale; trailing emotional thoughts dissolve into breathy vocal cord closure.
- **Micro-Cadence & Sentence Fragments**: Real humans speak in fragments, rhythmic pauses, and operative stress accents. Never write grammatically immaculate prose; write speakable, rhythmic cadence with one single stressed word in CAPS per beat.

## 8. Universal Audio Prompting Structure

When constructing the `AUDIO` section of production briefs, use this structured framework:

```markdown
### AUDIO & SOUND DESIGN
- **Acoustic Brief**: [Emotional target + listener perspective + primary focus].
- **Diegetic Hierarchy**:
  - Primary Anchor: [Dialogue line with vocal register / operative stressed word / breath cue OR primary hard transient].
  - Secondary Foley: [Specific material contact: Footwear × Substrate, Cloth fiber friction, Prop tactile mechanicals].
  - Environmental Bed: [Keynote sound + Soundmarks + RT60 reverberation profile + absorption characteristics].
- **Diegetic Status**: [Objective External vs. Subjective Meta-Diegetic vs. Trans-Diegetic].
- **Psychoacoustic Tools**: [Infrasound drone / Shepard tone / Pre-Drop silence / Anempathetic contrast / Distance HF roll-off].
- **Score & Musical Integration**: [Palette / BPM / Key alignment / The Drop point].
- **Negative Audio**: [Specific exclusions: no generic synth pads, no digital clipping, no robotic cadence, no background crowd babble].
```
