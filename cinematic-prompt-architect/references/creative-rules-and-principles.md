# Creative Ideation Rules, Roles & Advertising Principles

Great advertising is not born from random inspiration; it is engineered through disciplined creative friction, lateral thinking, and proven psychological principles. This reference defines the ideation engine used by the Creative Strategist subagent to produce breakthrough video concepts.

---

## 1. The Core Creative Axioms (Sullivan & Hegarty)

### Luke Sullivan (*Hey Whipple, Squeeze This*):
1. **Kill Your First Ten Ideas**: The first ideas out of your head are the same ones everyone else had — they are category clichés. Discard them immediately to reach original creative territory.
2. **If You Can Show It, Don't Say It**: The visual must carry the drama; the copy should only add a provocative twist. Never let voiceover describe what the camera is already showing.
3. **The Best Ads Don't Look Like Ads**: In an age of banner-blindness and ad-blockers, content that looks like a commercial gets skipped in 0.5 seconds. Make it look like a police investigation, a documentary confession, a comedic short film, or a high-tension drama.
4. **Find the Brand's Human Truth and Tell It with Wit**: Advertising is the intersection of a product's mechanical truth and a human being's messy, imperfect life.

### Sir John Hegarty (*Hegarty on Advertising*):
1. **When the World Zigs, ZAG**: The greatest danger in advertising is not offending people; it is being invisible. If every competitor is screaming, whisper. If everyone is colorful, go monochrome.
2. **Respect the Audience's Intelligence**: Never insult the viewer with spoon-fed logic. Leave a small gap in the narrative for the viewer's brain to leap across — that cognitive closure is what creates lasting memory.
3. **Simplicity is the Ultimate Sophistication**: A campaign with three messages delivers zero messages. Ruthlessly protect the Single-Minded Proposition (SMP).

## 2. Lateral Thinking in Advertising (Edward de Bono)

Traditional logical thought moves sequentially from premise to conclusion (vertical thinking). Lateral thinking makes sideways leaps to break established cognitive patterns:

### 1. Provocation & Po (Stepping Stones)
- State an absurd, legally impossible, or deliberately provocative premise to see where it leads:
  - *Provocation*: "What if our mattress brand encouraged people to wake up exhausted?"
  - *Creative Leap*: A commercial showing a couple having an all-night conversation, laughing hysterically because the bed is so comfortable they never want to leave it for sleep.

### 2. The Reversal Technique
- Take the goal and flip it 180 degrees:
  - Instead of showing how to use the product $\rightarrow$ Show the catastrophic comedy of errors when someone tries to survive *without* it.
  - Instead of celebrating the winner $\rightarrow$ Tell the story of the glorious runner-up (Avis: *"We're number two, so we try harder"*).

### 3. Random Input (Cross-Pollination)
- Introduce an unrelated object or genre into the product's world:
  - Combine a mundane household cleaner with the cinematic aesthetics of a 1970s gritty neo-noir mob interrogation.
  - Combine high-tech crypto software with the visual pacing of a 19th-century British nature documentary.

## 3. The SCAMPER Ideation Framework for Video Ads

| Letter | Technique | Video Ad Application |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **S** | **Substitute** | Swap the expected spokesperson (e.g. replace an energetic fitness influencer with an exhausted 45-year-old accountant who hates gyms). |
| **C** | **Combine** | Merge two opposing feelings or aesthetics (e.g. brutal horror film jump-scare pacing combined with baby shampoo). |
| **A** | **Adapt** | Steal a narrative structure from cinema or folklore (e.g. the Cinderella transformation, the Western standoff, the heist planning montage). |
| **M** | **Magnify / Minify** | Blow a micro-problem out of proportion to ridiculous epic stakes (e.g. a misplaced sock treated like an international geopolitical crisis). |
| **P** | **Put to Another Use** | Show the product used in a completely unexpected, extreme context (e.g. a rugged phone case used as a hockey puck). |
| **E** | **Eliminate** | Remove the voiceover completely; let 100% of the message land through visual choreography and pure Foley audio. |
| **R** | **Reverse** | Run the narrative backward in time (Memento style), starting from the peaceful result and rewinding to the chaotic origin. |

## 4. The "Enemy" & Strategic Tension Framework

Every legendary commercial has an **Enemy**. An ad without an enemy is just a self-congratulatory brochure. The enemy does not have to be a competing company; the best enemies are systemic, psychological, or cultural:

1. **The Behavioral Enemy**: Procrastination, doomscrolling, skipping meals, imposter syndrome.
2. **The Industry Lie**: The myth that skincare must cost $300; the lie that business software must require a 6-month enterprise onboarding.
3. **The Biological Friction**: Mid-afternoon energy crash, cold coffee, back pain at an office desk, squeaky floorboards waking the baby.
4. **The Social Absurdity**: Fake LinkedIn corporate speak, forced family small talk, performative wellness routines.

## 5. The 3-Angle Creative Mandate

Whenever presenting campaign concepts to the user, the subagent MUST always present **three contrasting creative archetypes**:

### Route A: The Contrarian Problem-First / Pattern Interrupt (Direct Response Focus)
- **Mindset**: Stops cold-traffic dead in their tracks within 0–2 seconds.
- **Tone**: Provocative, urgent, slightly irreverent, intellectually challenging.
- **Hook Mechanics**: Calls out a common mistake, debunks an industry myth, or exposes a hidden cost.
- **Best For**: Performance marketing, paid social ads, conversion-focused campaigns.

### Route B: The Emotional Human Truth (Brand Equity & Storytelling Focus)
- **Mindset**: Makes the viewer feel deeply seen and understood.
- **Tone**: Vulnerable, authentic, cinematic, grounded, warm or bittersweet.
- **Hook Mechanics**: Opens in the middle of a private, relatable human struggle or relationship tension.
- **Best For**: Brand positioning, premium offerings, high-trust high-ticket services.

### Route C: The Visceral Visual Metaphor / Demonstration (Show-Don't-Tell Focus)
- **Mindset**: Proves the product's superiority through an extreme physical test or surreal visual metaphor.
- **Tone**: Hyper-visual, visceral, tactile, jaw-dropping, kinetic.
- **Hook Mechanics**: Opens with a high-energy physical anomaly, destructive test, or surreal juxtaposition.
- **Best For**: Tangible physical products, tech performance claims, viral social sharing.
